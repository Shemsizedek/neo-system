# NEO-PACER FIN-006 — Chronology & Impossibility Audit

## Case
TVMLSM666 v. NEOLPS999

## Purpose
FIN-006 normalizes the historical, judicial, banking, succession, title, and custody record into one timeline and identifies anachronisms, impossible office dates, institution-name conflicts, duplicate events, retroactive validation, and chronology gaps.

The purpose is not to disprove a claim merely because it is unusual. The purpose is to identify every point where the asserted chain depends on a fact that must be chronologically possible before it can be authenticated.

## NEO chronology rule
Every event is assigned:
- asserted date;
- source date;
- institution then in existence;
- officeholder/capacity on that date;
- governing law then in force;
- predecessor/successor institution;
- instrument provenance;
- downstream references;
- contradiction flags;
- current forensic status.

A later document may corroborate that an earlier claim existed, but cannot by itself prove that the earlier transaction occurred.

## Primary chronology flags

### F-006-01 — 1949 event vs. later Certificate of Entitlement
The case record currently distinguishes an asserted 11 Aug. 1949 event from a later claimed CBP Certificate of Entitlement dated 4 Oct. 1976. They must not be treated as the same document unless the original instruments establish that relationship.

### F-006-02 — 1970 vs. 1976 certificate references
Claimant-side materials contain differing dates for a Certificate of Entitlement. Until the documents are compared, FIN-006 treats them as separate records or conflicting chronology, not interchangeable references.

### F-006-03 — 1971/1974 date anomaly
Later annulment-related materials contain a 21 Mar. 1971 reference while separately describing a 30-page Clarificatory Order dated 21 Mar. 1974. Since a 1971 order cannot clarify a 1972 decision, this is recorded as a transcription/date anomaly pending primary record inspection.

### F-006-04 — 1976 Supreme Court issuance gap
Fred Ruiz Castro's position as Chief Justice in Aug. 1976 is chronologically compatible. However, office compatibility is not issuance proof. The missing bridge remains an official Supreme Court docket/rollo/promulgation record tied to the disputed instrument.

### F-006-05 — Retroactive authority risk
Later appointments, presidencies, offices, corporate names, successor entities, or statutes cannot retroactively supply authority to earlier instruments. Each signatory must have held the relevant capacity at the exact execution date.

### F-006-06 — Successor-entity naming
A later successor institution may preserve or assume records/liabilities, but the successor's modern name must not be projected backward into an earlier transaction unless the document is explicitly a later reissue, certification, or archive copy.

### F-006-07 — Duplicate bullion events
Repeated quantities, certificates, SKRs, gold guarantees, trust certificates and account references may represent the same underlying asset. FIN-006 cross-links to FIN-003: no quantity is added to a consolidated total unless the timeline proves a distinct asset event.

### F-006-08 — Later publication is not contemporaneous proof
2010s and later websites, affidavits, attestation letters, videos and successor publications are evidence of later claims and continuity. They do not by themselves authenticate events asserted to have occurred decades earlier.

## Impossibility audit categories
The following are automatic escalation triggers:

1. **Office-date impossibility** — named signatory did not hold the claimed office on the document date.
2. **Institution-date impossibility** — institution did not yet exist under the asserted identity.
3. **Statutory anachronism** — document invokes a law, code, agency or legal regime not yet in effect.
4. **Technology anachronism** — form, identifier, payment rail, printing process, SWIFT field, barcode, digital format, or terminology postdates the instrument.
5. **Geographic/jurisdiction conflict** — person or institution demonstrably lacked territorial or subject-matter capacity.
6. **Duplicate-asset chronology** — same bullion lot appears simultaneously in incompatible locations or ownership states.
7. **Retroactive validation** — later document is used as the sole proof of an earlier transaction.
8. **Successor mismatch** — liability is attributed to a successor without evidence that the specific obligation transferred.
9. **Judicial sequence conflict** — order predates the judgment it allegedly clarifies/enforces, or enforcement predates finality/service.
10. **Document-generation anomaly** — typography, seal, logo, statutory citation, identifier or institutional nomenclature is inconsistent with the claimed date.
