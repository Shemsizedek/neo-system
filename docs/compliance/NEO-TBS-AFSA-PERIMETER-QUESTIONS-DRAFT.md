## Draft correspondence

Dear AIFC Client Office,

Thank you for your November 10, 2026 response outlining the distinction between AIFC entity registration and authorisation and explaining the potential FinTech Lab route.

We are undertaking a narrowly scoped **technical research proof of concept** for a proposed voluntary framework called the True Bitcoin Standard. This is a proposal for independent technical assessment, not a government-adopted standard or authorised financial benchmark.

For the present research-only phase:
- We use offline **synthetic** BTC, Counterparty XCP and NOMNI observation records and a documented calculation method.
- The demonstration is non-custodial, non-executable and not offered as a live price or settlement rate.
- It accepts no customer funds; does not create, issue, redeem or transfer tokens; does not conduct trading or payments; and does not onboard customers.
- It makes no representation of verified reserves, guaranteed convertibility, regulatory authorisation or governmental endorsement.
- No AIFC company has been created or regulatory application filed for this research work as a result of this inquiry.
- Any future NOMNI issuance, trading, redemption, reserve-backed claim, money transmission or custody is **outside** the proposed POC and would be evaluated separately.

To avoid incorrectly interpreting the AIFC perimeter, we would appreciate procedural clarification on these questions:

1. For **offline, purely technical and synthetic research** undertaken outside the AIFC, is any AIFC entity registration or AFSA application required merely to request a preliminary technical discussion? Are there any circumstances under which publishing a technical paper or code example changes that answer?
2. If a future phase publishes **real market observations or a reference index**, but continues to avoid investment advice, customer custody, payment processing, token issuance and trade execution, what factors would determine whether AFSA authorisation or financial-promotion restrictions apply? Is there a specific guidance section or activity classification you recommend reviewing?
3. Which factors establish that a foreign operator is carrying on an activity **in or from the AIFC**, particularly for an internationally accessible research website?
4. Does requesting an informal scope/perimeter meeting entail a fee or constitute entry into the February 2026 single-stage FinTech Lab application process? If not, what supporting technical materials would be useful for a non-binding preliminary discussion?
5. If we later propose a genuinely supervised real-user pilot, which **specific tests of innovation, user protections, operator presence, funding for approximately 12 months of operation, AML/CFT procedures and exit arrangements** would need to be evidenced, and when would payment of fees and entity registration be required?
6. Would different activities (a reference benchmark, customer payment service, digital-asset trading venue, token-backed redemption and third-party custody) need separately scoped classifications and approvals? We do not ask for an advance ruling on those activities today.
7. Which version of the official FinTech Lab fees, Rules and application instructions should a potential applicant use after the February 16, 2026 one-stage process change?

We are seeking guidance to define a lawful research boundary before considering any incorporation or paid application. We understand the Client Office cannot guarantee AFSA authorisation or provide binding legal advice. We would not describe this inquiry as approval or an endorsement.
