## 3. Controlling Philippine testamentary law

Republic Act No. 386, the Civil Code of the Philippines, was effective by 1952. For a non-holographic will, Articles 804–806 require, among other things:

- the will must be in writing and in a language or dialect known to the testator;
- execution/subscription by the testator;
- attestation and subscription by at least three credible witnesses in the presence of the testator and one another;
- page-signing and numbering requirements;
- an attestation clause stating the required execution facts; and
- acknowledgment before a notary public by the testator and witnesses.

Philippine probate law also provides that **no will passes real or personal estate unless it is proved and allowed in the proper court**. Probate allowance is the central due-execution gate.

Accordingly, notarization alone is not the terminal authentication event. The NEO Title Auditor must locate the probate or allowance proceeding, or determine whether another legally recognized succession mechanism is asserted.
