## Purpose

Promote the Sacred Chambers of Nun working material into a provenance-controlled educational module for GISS NEO LMS, NEO Oracle, NEOsync and NEO Algo.

### Logic & Noology 101
- Compare classical Freudian psychoanalysis with the project’s NEO-analysis framework.
- Identify the difference between intrapsychic explanations and structural/social explanations.
- Practice category discipline: doctrine, historical fact, clinical claim, legal claim and creative neology must remain distinct.

### History & Ourstory 101
- Study the historical development of concubinage, plural marriage and cuckold/cuckquean vocabulary.
- Compare household status systems across civilizations.
- Mark Ninsat and the Sacred Chambers framework as modern NEO constructions rather than ancient artifacts.

### Civics 101
- Study consent, household governance, plural-family responsibility, exit rights, transparency and dispute prevention.
- Distinguish advocacy of a relationship model from coercion or discrimination.

### Finance 101
- Develop a household budget for wives, Ninsat partners and children.
- Study bridal funds, dowry and dower history, inheritance planning and family capital.
- Introduce SEBABOND and DOWRYNOTE as project-defined Bitcoin/Counterparty-oriented accounting concepts, not automatically as regulated legal instruments.

### Law 101
- Separate religious covenant, private agreement, civil marriage, trust administration, family law and tokenization.
- Require jurisdiction-specific review before describing project instruments as enforceable securities, marital rights, ownership interests or guaranteed obligations.

### Rhetoric & Dialectics 101
- Study neology as deliberate cultural technology.
- Analyze the Temple Dirty Talk Initiative as a modern vocabulary-building project.
- Test rhetoric for dignity, clarity, consent and unsupported historical claims.

## Assessment standard

Students should be able to:
1. distinguish source-derived history from NEO synthesis;
2. explain the Sacred Chambers hierarchy accurately;
3. identify why Ninsat must not be presented as an attested ancient word;
4. analyze consent and power asymmetry in plural households;
5. model the economics of a large household with children;
6. explain the difference between a tokenized project record and an independently enforceable legal or financial instrument;
7. compare Freud’s psychoanalytic model with NEO-analysis without presenting NEO-analysis as established clinical science.
