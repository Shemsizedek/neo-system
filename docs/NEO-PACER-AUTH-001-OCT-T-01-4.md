# NEO-PACER AUTH-001 — OCT T-01-4 Provenance Dossier

**Case:** NEO-2026-GLOBAL-0001 — TVM-LSM-666 v. NEO-LPS-999  
**Node:** AUTH-001  
**Instrument:** Original Certificate of Title (OCT) T-01-4 / OCT 01-4  
**Current disposition:** **UNRESOLVED — MATERIAL ROOT-TITLE CLAIM**  
**Purpose:** Determine whether OCT T-01-4 exists as an independently authenticatable Philippine land-registration instrument, what it legally covers, who the registered owner was, and whether later instruments validly derive from it.

## 1. Claimant-side instrument profile

Materials in the TVM/Tallano claim ecosystem describe OCT T-01-4 as the root title for the "Hacienda Filipina Archipelago." Recurring attributes include:

- an asserted 1764 British/Royal Audiencia antecedent or "Protocol 01-4";
- later registration under Philippine Land Registration Act No. 496;
- Court of Land Registration Case No. 475;
- Decree No. 297 dated October 3, 1904;
- registered ownership attributed to Prince Lacan Acuña Tagean/Tallano;
- a claimed cadastral confirmation in 1914;
- derivative titles TCT T-408 and TCT T-498;
- later reliance on LRC/Civil Case No. 3957-P.

This profile is **source-derived from claimant/affiliated materials**. It is not adopted as a finding merely because it is repeated across publications.

## 2. Independent official-record findings

### A. Philippine Supreme Court E-Library — Lagcao v. Judge Gako

The Supreme Court's 2007 administrative resolution in **A.M. No. RTJ-04-1840** reproduces allegations from a litigant that the Tallano Estate owned lands covered by OCT T-01-4 and that the title was connected to Land Registration Case No. 475, Decree No. 297, and Civil/LRC Case No. 3957-P. The same decision also records the trial judge's statement that he had **"serious doubts"** about the association's claim that the land belonged to the Tallano Estate.

**NEO interpretation:** This is **P1 evidence that the T-01-4 theory entered real Philippine litigation**, but it is **not P1 authentication of the title itself**. The Supreme Court was deciding an administrative matter, not adjudicating the validity of OCT T-01-4.

Official source: https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44135/feed/

### B. Philippine Supreme Court / Lawphil — Lihaylihay v. Treasurer of the Philippines

**G.R. No. 192223 (2018)** reproduces a petitioner's allegations involving Tallano gold, Civil/LRC Case No. 3957-P, and related historical claims.

**NEO interpretation:** This independently proves that those allegations were formally presented in Supreme Court litigation. It does **not** establish that the Court found the underlying title, gold, or succession claims true.

Official sources:
- https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/64362
- https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2018/jul2018/gr_192223_2018.html

### C. Philippine Supreme Court / Lawphil — Rhema International Livelihood Foundation v. Hibix

**G.R. Nos. 225353-54 (2019)** records that a party attempted to take possession of property using a special writ of execution purportedly connected to LRC Civil Case No. 3957-P. The record states that the Court of Appeals had already enjoined enforcement of the order, causing the presented writ to be treated as bogus in that factual history. The Supreme Court's ultimate ruling concerned forcible entry and expressly distinguished physical possession from legal title.

**NEO interpretation:** This is a significant **adverse enforcement-history node**. It does not by itself disprove OCT T-01-4, but it shows that at least one purported downstream enforcement instrument tied to 3957-P was not legally operative.

Official source: https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2019/aug2019/gr_225353-54_2019.html

### D. Property Registration Decree — Presidential Decree No. 1529

The Supreme Court E-Library publishes **P.D. No. 1529**, which codifies Philippine property registration. Section 40 states that an Original Certificate of Title is entered, numbered, dated, signed, and sealed by the Register of Deeds and takes effect upon entry.

**NEO interpretation:** Any authentication of OCT T-01-4 must ultimately resolve to the competent land-registration archive, decree book, registry entry, and traceable registered-owner record. A reproduced image or later affidavit is not a substitute for registry provenance.

Official source: https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/11/55198

## 3. External adverse pleading

A published copy of the Republic's **Petition for Annulment of Judgment** concerning Civil Case No. 3957-P challenges the claimed OCT T-01-4 narrative, including the proposition that a 1764 instrument could itself have been a Torrens title and the provenance of later reconstitution materials.

**NEO classification:** External adversarial evidence, useful for contradiction testing but not treated as a final adjudication unless the official pleading and resulting judgment are authenticated.

Research copy: https://www.scribd.com/document/237514225/Petition-for-Annulment-of-Judgment

## 5. Authentication gates

AUTH-001 cannot be marked VERIFIED until the following are resolved:

1. **Registry existence** — Does the Land Registration Authority / competent Register of Deeds hold an official record for OCT T-01-4 or OCT 01-4?
2. **Decree book** — Does Decree No. 297 dated October 3, 1904 exist, and what land, case, and registered owner does it actually identify?
3. **Case record** — Does Court of Land Registration Case No. 475 exist in the asserted form?
4. **Survey/cadastral record** — Can Plan II-69 / PSU-2031 or related cadastral references be authenticated and geographically reconciled?
5. **Derivative title test** — Do TCT T-408 and TCT T-498 exist in official registry records, and do they actually derive from AUTH-001?
6. **Chain-of-custody** — Can the purported certified copies be traced to an issuing registrar, date, book/page, and archive location?
7. **Geographic scope** — Does the authenticated instrument, if it exists, cover specific parcels or the scope claimed by proponents?
8. **Legal continuity** — What effect, if any, did later land-registration laws, reconstitution proceedings, annulment proceedings, and appellate injunctions have on the instrument?

## 6. Current NEO finding

**F-AUTH-001.1:** The existence of a long-running OCT T-01-4 claim tradition is established.

**F-AUTH-001.2:** Official Philippine Supreme Court records establish that OCT T-01-4, Decree No. 297, Case No. 475, and Civil/LRC Case No. 3957-P have been asserted in actual litigation.

**F-AUTH-001.3:** Those Supreme Court records do not presently constitute an adjudication authenticating OCT T-01-4 as the root title to the Philippine archipelago.

**F-AUTH-001.4:** Downstream enforcement history is contested; at least one writ purportedly linked to 3957-P was reported in Supreme Court litigation as non-operative because enforcement had been enjoined.

**F-AUTH-001.5:** Root-title authentication therefore remains **UNRESOLVED**, with the Land Registration Authority / Register of Deeds / decree-book record as the controlling evidentiary target.

## 7. Investigator order

The NEO Title Auditor shall obtain or verify, in priority order:

- official certification for OCT T-01-4 / OCT 01-4;
- Decree No. 297 and its decree-book entry;
- CLR/CLRO/LRC Case No. 475 docket or archival index;
- TCT T-408 and TCT T-498 registry entries;
- Plan II-69 / PSU-2031 and cadastral record references;
- the complete certified record of Civil/LRC Case No. 3957-P and subsequent appellate injunction/annulment proceedings.

No downstream claim dependent upon AUTH-001 shall be elevated to PROVEN solely on the basis of reproduced claimant-side copies while these registry gates remain open.
