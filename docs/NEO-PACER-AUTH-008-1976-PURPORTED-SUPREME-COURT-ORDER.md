# NEO-PACER AUTH-008 — Purported 11 August 1976 Supreme Court Order / Writ of Execution

**Case:** NEO-2026-GLOBAL-0001  
**Instrument:** Purported Supreme Court Order dated 11 August 1976  
**Claimed signatories:** Chief Justice Fred Ruiz Castro and Judge Enrique A. Agana  
**Claimed underlying matter:** LRC/Civil Case No. 3957-P  
**Audit status:** UNRESOLVED — CLAIMANT-SIDE DOCUMENTARY EXISTENCE CORROBORATED / SUPREME COURT DOCKET, ISSUANCE AUTHORITY, SIGNATURE, AND LEGAL-EFFECT AUTHENTICATION PENDING

## 1. Claim under examination

Claimant-side Acopiado materials expressly describe an `ORIGINAL Supreme Court Order for Writ of Execution in Favor of Don Anacleto Madrigal Acopiado Sr – August 11, 1976`. The same materials reproduce text attributed to an instrument styled as a Supreme Court order for writ of execution, possession, and demolition and state that it bears the signatures of Chief Justice Fred Ruiz Castro and Pasay judge Enrique A. Agana.

This dossier records those statements as evidence of the **asserted instrument and its circulated contents**. They are not, standing alone, proof that the Supreme Court of the Philippines issued the instrument.

## 2. Source-derived content

### A. Acopiado Estate publication

The Acopiado Estate court-decisions index lists an `ORIGINAL Supreme Court Order for Writ of Execution ... August 11, 1976` in its asserted historical chain.

Its administrator affidavit and biography reproduce or paraphrase an instrument attributed to the Supreme Court and identify Fred Ruiz Castro and Enrique A. Agana as signatories. The asserted relief includes execution, possession and demolition and is tied to the earlier 1972 judgment and 1974 clarificatory order.

**Forensic classification:** claimant/estate-side provenance. Useful for locating the alleged document and testing internal continuity; not independent Supreme Court authentication.

### B. Republic / OSG annulment record

The reproduced Republic petition for annulment concerning Civil Case No. 3957-P identifies the 1972 decision, 1974 clarificatory order, 1975 decision, 1976 clarificatory decision, later writs, and reconstitution proceedings as disputed instruments. This establishes a substantial adverse procedural record surrounding the 3957-P document chain.

At the present research stage, the material reviewed does **not** supply an official Supreme Court docket number for an 11 August 1976 Supreme Court proceeding corresponding to the claimed order.

### C. Later Supreme Court litigation

Later official Philippine Supreme Court litigation concerning enforcement activity tied to LRC Civil Case No. 3957-P records that the Court of Appeals had enjoined enforcement of an order associated with that case and describes a later purported writ as bogus in the factual history of the dispute. That later ruling does not by itself determine the authenticity of the specific 11 August 1976 instrument, but it makes appellate-status and enforcement-history reconciliation compulsory.

## 3. Primary forensic questions

1. **Supreme Court docket identity:** What G.R., administrative, land-registration, or other Supreme Court docket generated the alleged 11 August 1976 order?
2. **Issuing tribunal:** Was the instrument actually issued by the Supreme Court, by the CFI/RTC, or assembled from language attributed to multiple tribunals?
3. **Dual-signature anomaly:** What procedural authority would support an order styled as a Supreme Court order bearing both the Chief Justice's signature and the signature of a trial-level judge?
4. **Record-book provenance:** Does the order appear in the Supreme Court's original rollo, minute resolutions, entry books, promulgation records, or certified archival indices?
5. **Signature authentication:** Do the Castro and Agana signatures match authenticated contemporaneous exemplars, including placement, title, ink/typewriter sequence, and document format?
6. **Execution authority:** Did the Supreme Court directly issue the alleged writ of execution/possession/demolition, or would execution ordinarily have been remanded to or issued by the court of origin?
7. **Party and service record:** Who moved for the order, who received notice, and where are the motion, opposition, proof of service, and return?
8. **Relationship to 3957-P:** Does the alleged Supreme Court instrument identify a review proceeding from Civil Case 3957-P, and if so, where is the appellate petition and disposition connecting the two?
9. **International-jurisdiction language:** The circulated text attributes jurisdiction/effect involving the International Court of Justice, Court of Arbitration, Hague institutions, and an `International Security Council`. Each phrase must be compared against the competence and nomenclature of those institutions as of 1976.
10. **Later judicial treatment:** What did CA-G.R. SP No. 70014, CA-G.R. SP No. 104604, and any Supreme Court review ultimately hold about the original/reconstituted instruments and enforcement?

## 5. Contradiction / anomaly register

### C-008-01 — Missing docket bridge
The circulated claim identifies a Supreme Court order but the currently reviewed materials do not establish the exact Supreme Court docket through which Civil Case 3957-P reached that Court.

### C-008-02 — Dual judicial signatures
The alleged order is described as signed by both a Supreme Court Chief Justice and the trial judge. This is sufficiently unusual to require direct archival verification rather than inference from a reproduced copy.

### C-008-03 — Institutional-jurisdiction language
The circulated text attributes broad jurisdictional consequences involving several international institutions. The legal basis and institutional terminology must be independently verified against their constitutive instruments and 1976 jurisdiction.

### C-008-04 — Enforcement conflict
Later litigation documents injunctions and disputes concerning execution of 3957-P-related orders/writs. Existence, authenticity, finality and enforceability must therefore be separately scored.

### C-008-05 — Original versus reconstituted record
The 3957-P chain later became the subject of reconstitution and annulment proceedings. Any copy of the 11 August 1976 instrument must be classified by generation and custody history before evidentiary weight is assigned.
