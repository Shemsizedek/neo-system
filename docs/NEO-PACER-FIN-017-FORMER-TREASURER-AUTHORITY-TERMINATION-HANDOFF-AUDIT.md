# NEO-PACER FIN-017 — Former Treasurer Authority Termination & Treasury Handoff Audit

## Purpose
Establish an auditable control framework for termination of a former Treasurer’s authority, transfer of treasury custody, preservation of records, and investigation of any alleged misconduct without treating allegations as established facts.

## Source basis
FIN-017 follows FIN-016. The reviewed governance materials distinguish the Chancellor’s nine-year election cycle from shorter terms applicable to other offices. The source record also provides governance mechanisms for removal, vacancies, and management authority. The Noone Society organizational materials identify treasury-related entities including Noone Commonwealth Fund, Nu Omni Bank, Nu Omni Credit Union, and Noone Crown Treasury.

## Core control principle
Termination of office and proof of misconduct are separate issues. A former Treasurer’s authority should be evaluated from the effective governance action—expiration, removal, resignation, replacement, or other valid vacancy event—while any allegation of sabotage, diversion, concealment, destruction, unauthorized access, or interference must be independently established by evidence.

## Audit objectives
1. Determine the effective date and instrument terminating or superseding the former Treasurer’s authority.
2. Identify every treasury account, institution, wallet, platform, ledger, repository, credential, signing authority, contract, record set, device, seal, key, token, and other asset under the Treasurer’s custody or control.
3. Determine whether access and signing authority were revoked or transferred promptly after the governance event.
4. Reconcile balances and transactions across the transition window.
5. Preserve historical records and establish chain of custody for evidence.
6. Identify unresolved access, custody, reconciliation, or governance exceptions.
7. Separate verified facts, documentary evidence, allegations, and investigative conclusions.

## Treasury scope
The audit inventory should expressly test for records or assets associated with:
- Noone Commonwealth Fund
- Nu Omni Bank
- Nu Omni Credit Union
- Noone Crown Treasury
- Any subsidiary, affiliate, family organization, ministry, temple, tribunal, trust, fund, merchant account, payment processor, digital asset wallet, or other treasury vehicle operated for or on behalf of Noone Society

Inclusion in this scope is an audit-control measure and does not by itself establish the present legal or operational status of any named entity.

## Authority termination record
For each former Treasurer or treasury custodian, record:
- Full office/title
- Appointment or election instrument
- Date authority began
- Term applicable to the office
- Removal, resignation, expiration, replacement, disability, death, or other termination event
- Effective date and time of termination, if documented
- Governing resolution, minutes, notice, order, or other instrument
- Successor or interim custodian
- Date notice was delivered to the former officer
- Date notice was delivered to financial institutions and service providers
- Date each access right was actually revoked

Where the effective termination date cannot be established from authenticated records, mark the field UNRESOLVED rather than infer a date.

## Access revocation matrix
For every treasury system, test and document:
- Bank/account signatory authority
- Online banking credentials
- Payment processors
- Debit/credit cards
- ACH/wire permissions
- Check stock and check-signing authority
- Accounting software
- Tax/payment portals
- Email accounts used for treasury operations
- Cloud storage and document repositories
- Password managers
- Multi-factor authentication devices/numbers
- API keys and application credentials
- Cryptocurrency or digital-asset wallets, seed phrases, hardware wallets, and multisignature authority
- Physical keys, safes, seals, stamps, books, and records
- Vendor and contractual authority

Status for each item: REVOKED / TRANSFERRED / NOT APPLICABLE / UNRESOLVED.

## Handoff package
A complete treasury handoff should include, where applicable:
1. Opening and closing balance statements.
2. General ledger and subsidiary ledgers.
3. Bank statements and reconciliations.
4. Outstanding checks, deposits, receivables, payables, and liabilities.
5. Contracts, subscriptions, recurring payments, and vendor files.
6. Tax and regulatory records.
7. Donation, contribution, grant, membership, and restricted-fund records.
8. Asset registers and custody records.
9. Digital wallet addresses and transaction histories.
10. Credentials transferred through a secure process rather than embedded in the audit report.
11. Pending disputes, chargebacks, claims, liens, or collection matters.
12. A signed custody-transfer acknowledgment when available.

## Transition-window reconciliation
Use a defined forensic window surrounding the authority transition. At minimum compare:
- Last reconciled balance before transition
- Transactions initiated before termination but posted afterward
- Transactions initiated after termination
- Changes to beneficiaries, vendors, payees, mailing addresses, recovery emails, telephone numbers, passwords, MFA methods, or account administrators
- New accounts, closed accounts, withdrawals, transfers, wires, ACH transactions, checks, card transactions, digital-asset transfers, refunds, reversals, and cash movements
- Deleted, altered, missing, or inaccessible records

Any anomaly must be classified as VERIFIED EXCEPTION, EXPLAINED/RESOLVED, or UNRESOLVED. An anomaly is not automatically evidence of sabotage or fraud.

## Evidence preservation
Preserve original records wherever possible. For digital evidence, retain source files, timestamps, metadata, transaction identifiers, account identifiers, export dates, and cryptographic hashes when practical. Maintain a chain-of-custody log showing who obtained, copied, reviewed, transferred, or stored each evidentiary item.

Do not overwrite original files during review. Analytical copies should be separately identified.

## Alleged sabotage or misconduct track
Any sabotage allegation must be maintained as a separate investigative track. For each allegation record:
- Specific act alleged
- Date/time or estimated period
- System/account affected
- Source of allegation
- Documentary or technical evidence supporting it
- Evidence contradicting it
- Financial or operational impact
- Persons with access during the relevant period
- Alternative explanations considered
- Current evidentiary classification

Recommended classifications: ALLEGATION ONLY / CORROBORATED / CONTRADICTED / INCONCLUSIVE / VERIFIED.

No adverse factual conclusion should be entered solely because an individual formerly held treasury authority.

## Immediate control actions
Where not already completed:
1. Freeze or remove obsolete signing and administrative authority.
2. Rotate passwords, MFA factors, API keys, recovery contacts, and sensitive credentials.
3. Notify institutions of the currently authorized treasury officers through their required procedures.
4. Preserve statements, logs, emails, ledgers, and transaction exports before routine retention systems delete them.
5. Inventory physical and digital treasury property.
6. Reconcile all accounts through the effective transition date and forward.
7. Document unresolved property or records not surrendered.
8. Require dual control for high-risk treasury actions during the transition period.

## Relationship to FIN-016
FIN-016 establishes that, under each plausible 2015 source-date anchor reviewed, the second nine-year period began in 2024, while the exact controlling anniversary remains unresolved pending authentication of governance records. FIN-017 therefore does not presume that the nine-year cycle alone terminated the Treasurer’s authority. It requires the specific governance instrument applicable to that office and transition.

## Current finding
The reviewed source framework supports conducting a formal authority-termination and treasury-handoff audit. It does not, standing alone, establish that any named former Treasurer committed sabotage, fraud, diversion, destruction of records, or unauthorized post-termination activity. Those conclusions require transaction-level, access-log, custody, communications, and governance evidence.
