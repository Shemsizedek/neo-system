# NEO-PACER AUTH-005 — 1972 Judgment / Compromise Agreement

**Case:** NEO-2026-GLOBAL-0001 — TVM-LSM-666 v. NEO-LPS-999  
**Instrument under review:** Purported Decision with Compromise Agreement dated 4 February 1972, LRC/Civil Case No. 3957-P, Court of First Instance of Rizal, Branch XXVIII, Pasay City, attributed to Judge Enrique A. Agana.  
**Current disposition:** **UNRESOLVED — EXISTENCE OF THE CLAIMED JUDICIAL INSTRUMENT IS STRONGLY CORROBORATED IN LATER OFFICIAL LITIGATION; AUTHENTICITY, ORIGINAL CONTENTS, JURISDICTION, FINALITY, AND PRESENT LEGAL EFFECT REMAIN CONTESTED.**

## 1. Claim architecture

Claimant-side copies present a lengthy 4 February 1972 “Decision with Compromise Agreement” in LRC/Civil Case No. 3957-P. The reproduced caption describes an action for quieting of titles, reconveyance of real properties, and reconstitution of OCT T-01-4, TCT T-408 and TCT T-498. Reproduced text further asserts that the National Government entered a compromise with Tallano interests and contains broad land-reconveyance and related provisions.

The forensic question is not merely whether a document bearing this title circulates. The controlling questions are: (1) whether the 4 February 1972 decision was actually promulgated by the competent court; (2) what the authentic original text and dispositive portion were; (3) whether all named parties, including the Republic, validly consented to any compromise; (4) whether the court possessed subject-matter and personal/in-rem jurisdiction for every relief purportedly granted; (5) whether the decision became final and executory; (6) whether subsequent reconstitution proceedings accurately restored the original record; and (7) what later appellate orders did to enforcement or legal effect.

## 2. Official judicial corroboration

### 2.1 Lagcao v. Judge Gako — Supreme Court E-Library

The Supreme Court’s official administrative decision records that a litigant later relied on LRC/Civil Case No. 3957-P and attached a xeroxed copy of a decision dated 4 February 1972, an entry of judgment allegedly showing finality on 4 April 1972, writs of execution, an oath of office as Judicial Administrator, letters of administration, later orders and a certificate of finality. The Supreme Court did not authenticate the underlying Tallano ownership theory; the quoted trial judge stated that he had “serious doubts” about the claimed Tallano Estate ownership. This is strong official corroboration that the 1972 instrument was invoked in real litigation, but not proof that every proposition within the copy was genuine or legally effective.

Source: https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44135

### 2.2 Lihaylihay v. Treasurer of the Philippines — Supreme Court E-Library

The Supreme Court’s 2018 decision reproduces the petitioner’s allegation that on 4 February 1972 Judge Enrique B. Agana, in LRC/Civil Case No. 3957-P, ordered President Marcos to return alleged gold bullion and diamonds to the Central Bank. The Court’s reproduction verifies that this allegation was formally presented in litigation; it does not amount to a judicial finding that the alleged bullion, ownership, or 1972 order was authentic.

Source: https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/64362

### 2.3 Rhema International Livelihood Foundation v. Hibix — Supreme Court E-Library

The Supreme Court’s 2019 decision records later attempted enforcement of a writ associated with LRC Civil Case No. 3957-P and notes that the Court of Appeals had already enjoined enforcement of the relevant order as early as 25 June 2002. This is material adverse enforcement-history evidence. It does not itself decide the authenticity of the 1972 document, but it proves that downstream enforcement was judicially contested and restricted.

Source: https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/65589

## 3. Later Court of Appeals controversy

The Republic later brought CA-G.R. SP No. 70014 seeking annulment of judgments/orders arising from the reconstitution history of Civil Case No. 3957-P. A publicly reproduced Court of Appeals decision in consolidated CA-G.R. SP Nos. 70014 and 104604 describes the Republic’s position that the challenged reconstitution proceedings and related records were jurisdictionally defective, and records extensive disputes over the identities of parties, the existence and contents of earlier decisions, notice/publication, and alleged extrinsic fraud.

The Court of Appeals material is critical because it requires NEO-PACER to distinguish between:

1. an original 1972 court record, if any;
2. post-fire reconstitution proceedings after destruction of Pasay City Hall records;
3. later copies, certifications, writs, and derivative instruments; and
4. whether a ruling invalidating reconstitution also invalidated the underlying original judgment, as opposed to the later reconstruction/enforcement process.

A definitive finding requires the certified Court of Appeals rollo and dispositive portion, not advocacy summaries from either claimant or government-aligned sources.

A 137-page reproduction titled “Decision with Compromise Agreement” is publicly available and bears the caption of the Court of First Instance of Rizal, Seventh Judicial District, Branch XXVIII, Pasay City, LRC/Civil Case No. 3957-P. It contains provisions purporting to require reconveyance of numerous properties and to recognize broad interests of Tallano/Acop claimants. Because the presently located copy is hosted by non-official sites, it is classified as a research copy pending comparison against the authenticated judicial record.

## 5. Major contradiction / authenticity flags

### CF-005-A — Same-date filing and decision issue

The later Court of Appeals record, as reproduced publicly, questions why the purported 4 February 1972 Decision with Compromise Agreement bears the exact same date as the filing of the purported amended petition. This is an internal chronology issue requiring original docket timestamps, minutes, hearings, submissions, and promulgation records.

### CF-005-B — Reconstitution versus original-record distinction

The Republic’s later annulment theory attacks the jurisdiction and integrity of reconstitution proceedings and certain derivative records. NEO-PACER must not automatically infer from that attack either that the original 1972 decision never existed or that it was valid. The original record and the later reconstruction must be authenticated independently.

### CF-005-C — Subject-matter jurisdiction

The Republic argued that an ordinary civil action for quieting/recovery could not lawfully function as a land-registration proceeding capable of reconstituting Torrens titles. This issue goes directly to legal effect even if the signature and physical document are authentic.

### CF-005-D — Notice and publication

The annulment materials argue that the reconstitution/title process lacked statutory notice and publication to occupants, adjoining owners and other interested parties. For an instrument allegedly affecting extraordinarily broad land areas, due-process compliance is a high-materiality jurisdictional question.

### CF-005-E — Enforcement injunction

Official Supreme Court litigation confirms that the Court of Appeals enjoined enforcement of at least later 3957-P orders/writs. Any tribunal conclusion that the 1972 judgment remains presently enforceable must reconcile the entire appellate history.

## 6. Authentication requirements

AUTH-005 cannot be upgraded to VERIFIED until the following are obtained and compared:

- Certified original or archival certified copy of the 4 February 1972 decision from the proper Philippine court/archive;
- complete original case docket sheet for LRC/Civil Case No. 3957-P;
- filing date of the amended petition and all pleadings allegedly resolved in the decision;
- stenographic notes/minutes of hearings leading to the compromise;
- signed compromise agreement, with signatures and authority of all parties and counsel;
- Office of the Solicitor General authority and records showing Republic participation/approval;
- proof of any presidential approval or waiver actually relied upon;
- entry of judgment and proof of service establishing claimed finality on 4 April 1972;
- original writs of execution and sheriff returns;
- Pasay City court fire/destruction inventory and reconstitution petition record;
- certified CA-G.R. SP No. 70014 / 104604 decision, resolutions, injunctions, and finality records;
- any Supreme Court review of that Court of Appeals disposition;
- side-by-side forensic comparison of original, reconstituted, and internet-circulated copies.

## 7. NEO forensic finding

**Finding AUTH-005-F1:** The existence of a claimed 4 February 1972 judgment in LRC/Civil Case No. 3957-P is not merely an internet rumor. Philippine Supreme Court decisions independently establish that such a decision and related 3957-P materials were invoked in later litigation.

**Finding AUTH-005-F2:** Those later Supreme Court decisions do not authenticate the truth of the Tallano title, gold, or global-asset allegations contained in litigants’ pleadings.

**Finding AUTH-005-F3:** The legal effect of the purported 1972 instrument is materially complicated by later reconstitution litigation, jurisdictional objections, claims of falsification/extrinsic fraud, and Court of Appeals restraints on enforcement.

**Finding AUTH-005-F4:** NEO-PACER therefore classifies the instrument as **UNRESOLVED WITH STRONG OFFICIAL LITIGATION CORROBORATION**, pending primary judicial-record authentication and reconciliation of the appellate history.
