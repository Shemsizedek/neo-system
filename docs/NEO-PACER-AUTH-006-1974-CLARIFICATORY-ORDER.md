# NEO-PACER AUTH-006 — 1974 Clarificatory Order

**Case:** NEO-2026-GLOBAL-0001 / TVM Affidavit investigation  
**Instrument:** Purported Clarificatory Order, LRC/Civil Case No. 3957-P  
**Asserted date:** 21 March 1974  
**Asserted issuer:** Judge Enrique A. Agana, Court of First Instance of Rizal, Branch XXVIII, Pasay City  
**Current disposition:** **UNRESOLVED — DOCUMENTARY EXISTENCE STRONGLY CORROBORATED / PRIMARY COURT-RECORD AUTHENTICATION AND LATER APPELLATE EFFECT PENDING**

## 1. Scope

AUTH-006 tests whether the instrument identified in later records as the **Clarificatory Order dated March 21, 1974, consisting of approximately 30 pages** was genuinely issued in LRC/Civil Case No. 3957-P, what it actually clarified, whether it altered the February 4, 1972 Decision with Compromise Agreement, and what later proceedings did to its legal effect.

The audit deliberately separates: (a) documentary existence, (b) authorship/signature, (c) entry in the original court docket, (d) jurisdiction, (e) finality, (f) execution, and (g) later appellate treatment.

## 2. Source-derived findings

### A. Republic/OSG annulment record

A reproduced **Petition for Annulment of Judgment** in **CA-G.R. SP No. 70014**, captioned *Republic of the Philippines v. Regional Trial Court of Pasay City, Branch 111, et al.*, specifically lists as **Annex B** a **Clarificatory Order dated March 21, 1974 consisting of 30 pages, allegedly rendered by Judge Enrique Agana**. The same pleading separately lists the February 4, 1972 Decision with Compromise Agreement, November 4, 1975 Decision, and January 19, 1976 Clarificatory Decision.

This is strong evidence that by the annulment litigation the March 21, 1974 instrument was a concrete, identified document in the disputed 3957-P record. It is **not** by itself proof that the instrument was genuine or legally valid, because the Republic expressly challenged the authenticity/jurisdiction of the group of instruments.

### B. Claimant-side chronology

A separate claimant-side litigation compilation identifies the same sequence: February 4, 1972 Decision with Compromise Agreement; June 14, 1972 Entry of Judgment; **March 21, 1974 Clarificatory Order issued by Judge Enrique A. Agana**; September 10, 1974 Writ of Execution, Demolition and Possession; November 1974 sheriff return; November 4, 1975 Decision; and January 19, 1976 Clarificatory Decision.

This provides cross-ecosystem documentary continuity as to the asserted date, issuer, and placement in the chain, but it does not substitute for the original docket, signature, seal, or certified court copy.

### C. Date normalization issue

One reproduced section of the annulment pleading later refers to a **“Clarificatory Order dated March 21, 1971”** while the pleading's annex schedule identifies **March 21, 1974**. The 1971 reference is facially inconsistent with the instrument being described as clarifying the 1972 decision and should be treated as a likely transcription/typographical anomaly unless the original pleading and annex prove otherwise.

NEO-PACER therefore normalizes the investigative target to **March 21, 1974**, while preserving the conflicting 1971 reference as a contradiction flag rather than silently correcting the historical record.

## 3. Forensic issues

### AUTH-006-A — Original docket provenance
Obtain the original docket entry/minute book for March 21, 1974 in LRC/Civil Case No. 3957-P and establish whether the order was entered contemporaneously.

### AUTH-006-B — Signature and judicial capacity
Compare the signature attributed to Judge Enrique A. Agana against authenticated judicial signatures from the same period. Establish his assignment and authority on the asserted date.

### AUTH-006-C — Page-set integrity
The Republic's later pleading describes the instrument as approximately **30 pages**. Obtain all pages, verify pagination, initials/signatures, stamps, certifications, staple/filing sequence, and whether pages were substituted or reproduced from later reconstitution packets.

### AUTH-006-D — Clarification versus modification
Determine clause-by-clause whether the 1974 instrument merely explained the February 1972 judgment or materially enlarged, altered, or supplied relief absent from that judgment. Any substantive modification after finality requires separate legal analysis.

### AUTH-006-E — September 1974 execution bridge
The documentary chain places a September 10, 1974 Writ of Execution, Demolition and Possession after the March order. The audit must determine whether that writ cites AUTH-006, what exact decretal provisions it purported to execute, and whether the sheriff return matches the same property/parties.

### AUTH-006-F — Later 1975/1976 instruments
The November 4, 1975 Decision and January 19, 1976 Clarificatory Decision must be compared against AUTH-006 to determine whether they confirm, supersede, reverse, modify, or contradict it.

### AUTH-006-G — CA-G.R. SP No. 70014 effect
Later annulment proceedings are indispensable. The Republic challenged the 1972, 1974, 1975 and 1976 instruments and the reconstitution proceedings. The final Court of Appeals disposition and any Supreme Court review must be obtained from official archives and mapped instrument-by-instrument. No present enforceability conclusion should be entered before that reconciliation.

## 4. Contradiction flags

1. **Date conflict:** March 21, 1974 in the annex schedule versus a later reproduced March 21, 1971 reference.
2. **Original versus reconstituted record:** later copies may derive from reconstitution proceedings rather than the contemporaneous 1974 court file.
3. **Clarification scope:** whether the instrument clarified or impermissibly expanded the 1972 judgment remains unresolved.
4. **Jurisdiction/notice:** the Republic later alleged jurisdictional defects in the reconstitution/title proceedings.
5. **Appellate effect:** later CA proceedings reportedly addressed the reconstitution and associated instruments; the official judgment and entry of judgment are required before legal-effect scoring.

### NEO finding

AUTH-006 advances the chain because the March 21, 1974 instrument is specifically identified in both claimant-side chronologies and the Republic's later annulment pleading. That is materially stronger than an unsupported internet assertion. However, the Republic's pleading labels it an alleged instrument and attacks the broader 3957-P/reconstitution architecture. Accordingly, NEO-PACER does not elevate the 1974 order to verified judicial authority until the original/certified court record and later appellate disposition are reconciled.
