# NEO-PACER AUTH-007 — 19 January 1976 Clarificatory Decision

**Case:** NEO-2026-GLOBAL-0001 — TVM-LSM-666 v. NEO-LPS-999  
**Instrument under review:** Purported Clarificatory Decision dated 19 January 1976 in LRC/Civil Case No. 3957-P  
**Status:** UNRESOLVED — DOCUMENTARY EXISTENCE STRONGLY CORROBORATED / PRIMARY COURT-RECORD AUTHENTICATION, PARTY-NOTICE, AND APPELLATE-EFFECT REVIEW PENDING

## 1. Investigative Question

Did Judge Enrique A. Agana validly issue a Clarificatory Decision on 19 January 1976 in LRC/Civil Case No. 3957-P, what did it actually modify or clarify, who received notice and an opportunity to be heard, and what legal effect—if any—survived the later reconstitution and appellate proceedings?

## 2. Source-Derived Findings

### 2.1 Existence of a specifically dated instrument is repeatedly documented
Claimant-side historical compilations consistently identify a **Clarificatory Decision dated 19 January 1976** following the 4 November 1975 decision and earlier 1972/1974 instruments. A modern Acopiado archive likewise lists an "ORIGINAL Clarificatory Order" dated 19 January 1976 in LRC/Civil Case No. 3957-P.

### 2.2 The Republic/OSG annulment record treats the document as a material claimed instrument
A reproduced Petition for Annulment of Judgment filed for the Republic in CA-G.R. SP No. 70014 discusses the alleged 19 January 1976 Clarificatory Decision and places it among the instruments later sought to be reconstituted or executed.

### 2.3 A major internal contradiction exists concerning notice and timing
Within the same reproduced annulment record, the Acopiado side is quoted as stating that it had **not been aware of the existence or rendition of the purported 19 January 1976 clarificatory decision**, that it had not been heard regarding it, and that a copy allegedly surfaced only in October 1998 attached to a later filing. If authenticated, this is a material due-process and provenance issue.

### 2.4 The January 1976 instrument is distinct from the November 1975 decision
The source record indicates a sequence of:
1. 4 Feb 1972 Decision with Compromise Agreement;
2. 21 Mar 1974 Clarificatory Order;
3. 4 Nov 1975 Decision;
4. 19 Jan 1976 Clarificatory Decision.

The January 1976 instrument therefore cannot be treated merely as another copy of the 1972 judgment. Its precise textual changes must be isolated.

### 2.5 Later Court of Appeals litigation is outcome-determinative
Secondary reproductions of the 11 December 2009 Court of Appeals disposition in CA-G.R. SP Nos. 70014 and 104604 report that the reconstitution proceedings were declared void for lack of jurisdiction and that the listed 3957-P instruments—including the 19 January 1976 Clarificatory Decision—were annulled/declared void and of no force and effect. NEO-PACER will not elevate that proposition to P1 until the certified Court of Appeals decision is obtained from an official judiciary source or authenticated court record.

## 3. Forensic Contradiction Flags

### CF-007-A — Late-Emergence / Provenance Flag
If a party to the underlying litigation first saw the purported January 1976 decision in 1998, determine where the original was maintained from 1976–1998, who possessed it, and whether it appears in contemporaneous docket books, minute books, mailing/service records, or execution records.

### CF-007-B — Notice and Opportunity-to-Be-Heard Flag
Identify every party whose substantive rights changed between the November 1975 decision and January 1976 clarification. Determine whether those parties received notice and were heard.

### CF-007-C — Clarification-versus-Modification Flag
A court may clarify an ambiguous judgment, but a post-finality instrument cannot simply rewrite substantive rights without legal authority. Conduct a line-by-line redline of the 4 Nov 1975 decision against the 19 Jan 1976 document to identify whether the latter merely explained existing terms or materially changed parties, property, relief, liabilities, or beneficiaries.

### CF-007-D — Reconstitution Flag
Determine whether the 19 Jan 1976 instrument itself was ever properly included in the formal reconstitution proceeding, separately from the 4 Nov 1975 decision. The reproduced annulment materials contain assertions that the 1976 instrument was not part of the original reconstitution effort and emerged later.

### CF-007-E — Appellate-Effect Flag
Obtain and authenticate the complete 11 Dec 2009 Court of Appeals decision in CA-G.R. SP Nos. 70014 and 104604, subsequent motions for reconsideration, entries of judgment, and any Supreme Court review. Present legal effect cannot be inferred from the historical document alone.

## 4. Required Authentication Evidence

1. Certified copy of the 19 January 1976 Clarificatory Decision from the originating court archive or lawful successor archive.
2. Contemporary docket and minute-book entries showing filing/rendition date.
3. Judge Enrique A. Agana signature exemplars and certification of signature/seal.
4. Clerk-of-court issuance and service records.
5. Complete 4 November 1975 Decision for redline comparison.
6. Motions or petitions that allegedly prompted the January 1976 clarification.
7. Proof of service and responses from every affected party.
8. Original/reconstituted record inventory showing whether this instrument was included and when.
9. Certified Court of Appeals record for CA-G.R. SP Nos. 70014 and 104604.
10. Supreme Court docket/orders, if any, reviewing that Court of Appeals disposition.

## 5. NEO Title Auditor Finding

The 19 January 1976 Clarificatory Decision has **strong documentary existence corroboration**, because it is repeatedly identified by date and function across adverse and claimant-side records. However, its **original provenance, contemporaneous notice, relationship to the 4 November 1975 decision, proper reconstitution, and surviving legal effect remain unresolved**.

**Disposition:** UNRESOLVED — DOCUMENTARY EXISTENCE STRONGLY CORROBORATED / PRIMARY COURT-RECORD AUTHENTICATION, PARTY-NOTICE, AND APPELLATE-EFFECT REVIEW PENDING.

## 6. NEO Rule Applied

Existence of a historical judicial instrument is analytically separate from authenticity, jurisdiction, due process, finality, enforceability, and present legal effect. NEO-PACER records each proposition independently.
