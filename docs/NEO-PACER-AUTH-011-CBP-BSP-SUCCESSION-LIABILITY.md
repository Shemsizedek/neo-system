# AUTH-011 — Central Bank of the Philippines → Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas Succession and Liability Audit

**Case:** NEO-2026-GLOBAL-0001 — TVM-LSM-666 v. NEO-LPS-999  
**Node:** 11 — Successor Liability  
**Status:** UNRESOLVED — STATUTORY SUCCESSION FRAMEWORK VERIFIED / SPECIFIC CLAIM TRANSFER OR RETENTION NOT YET ESTABLISHED

## Question Presented

If an alleged obligation, deposit, custodial liability, certificate, bullion account, or trust-related liability of the former Central Bank of the Philippines (CBP) were authenticated, did that specific liability transfer to the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP), remain with the Central Bank–Board of Liquidators (CB-BOL), get discharged or restructured, or otherwise cease to be enforceable?

## Verified statutory framework

Republic Act No. 7653 created the BSP and, in Section 132, required a Committee to determine which assets and liabilities of the former Central Bank would be transferred to or assumed by BSP. The statute did not provide for an automatic transfer of every Central Bank liability. It expressly required identification and selection of transferred items.

Section 132 further provided that any Central Bank asset or liability not transferred to BSP would remain with the Central Bank, which would continue as the CB Board of Liquidators for purposes of administering, disposing of, and liquidating retained assets and liabilities.

Administrative Order No. 100 (30 Nov 1993) approved the identified transfer of certain CB assets and the assumption by BSP of certain CB liabilities, including deposits, borrowings, and other liabilities enumerated in the attached balance sheets, annexes, and schedules. The transfer was effective 3 July 1993 and was subject to Commission on Audit review.

Executive Order No. 169 (17 Apr 1994) formally continued the juridical existence of the old Central Bank as the CB-BOL for retained assets and liabilities not transferred to BSP. Later executive orders reconstituted the CB-BOL.

Republic Act No. 11211 (2019) amended Section 132(b) but retained the statutory concept of continued liquidation of the former Central Bank's net liabilities.

## Supreme Court control

Philippine Supreme Court decisions concerning Banco Filipino confirm that BSP and CB-BOL are legally distinct destinations for former Central Bank obligations. The Court has recognized that transferred assets/liabilities went to BSP, while non-transferred assets/liabilities remained with CB-BOL. This means the proper defendant or obligor depends on the actual transfer schedule and the identity of the specific liability.

## NEO forensic rule

**Successor status alone does not prove assumption of a particular liability.**

For any TVM-LSM-666-related claim, the Title Auditor must identify the alleged liability by instrument/account/certificate number and trace it against the official Section 132 transfer schedules approved under Administrative Order No. 100.

## Mandatory authentication gates

1. Identify the precise alleged CBP liability: account number, certificate number, deposit type, trust designation, bullion quantity, creditor/beneficiary name, and booking date.
2. Obtain the complete Committee of Seven report referenced in Administrative Order No. 100, including the 50-page balance sheets, annexes, and schedules.
3. Determine whether the claimed liability appears among the liabilities expressly assumed by BSP.
4. If absent, determine whether it appears in the retained-liability records of CB-BOL.
5. Obtain Commission on Audit transfer/audit records relating to the identified item.
6. Trace any later write-off, set-off, compromise, restructuring, condonation, revalidation, payment, extinguishment, or transfer.
7. Determine limitations, final-judgment, sovereign-immunity, notice, and procedural defenses separately from substantive ownership.
8. Do not infer present liability from institutional succession language alone.

## Current finding

The legal succession architecture itself is **verified**: RA 7653 created a bifurcated transfer system in which some former CB assets and liabilities were transferred to BSP and others remained with the CB-BOL. Administrative Order No. 100 confirms that specific schedules governed the actual transfers.

What is **not yet established** is whether any alleged TVM-LSM-666 certificate, deposit, bullion custody obligation, account, or trust liability was listed in those transfer schedules or retained-liability records.

Therefore the present disposition is:

**UNRESOLVED — STATUTORY SUCCESSION FRAMEWORK VERIFIED / SPECIFIC CLAIM TRANSFER, RETENTION, DISCHARGE, OR PRESENT LIABILITY NOT YET ESTABLISHED.**
