# Shelton Estate & Co. — NEO Estates Property Scouting & Site-Fit Matrix

Status: Canonical acquisition-screening standard
Module: NEO-HOUSES-001
Version: 1.0

## Purpose
This matrix is the first-pass acquisition gate for houses, land, multifamily, businesses, warehouses and mixed-use properties considered for Shelton Estate & Co. NEO Estates.

It answers four questions:
1. Is the underlying asset fundamentally sound?
2. Which NEO Estate prototype best fits it?
3. What improvements are required?
4. Should Shelton Estate & Co. pursue, renegotiate, hold or reject the opportunity?

This is a screening framework, not a substitute for appraisal, inspection, title review, survey, environmental review, zoning analysis, licensed engineering, legal counsel or investment underwriting.

## Scoring System
Each category is scored 0–5.

- 5 = exceptional fit / low friction
- 4 = strong fit
- 3 = workable with moderate improvements
- 2 = weak fit / material remediation
- 1 = severe constraint
- 0 = disqualifying or unavailable

Weighted score = category score / 5 × category weight.

Total score = 100 points.

## Decision Bands
- **85–100 — Priority Acquisition:** advance to full underwriting immediately.
- **70–84 — Strong Candidate:** proceed subject to specific diligence items.
- **55–69 — Conditional / Renegotiate:** only proceed if price, terms or remediation solve weaknesses.
- **40–54 — Watch / Special Situation:** require unusual strategic value or creative financing.
- **Below 40 — Reject:** do not spend additional acquisition resources without a documented exception.

## Automatic Red Flags
A high total score does not override a critical red flag.

Automatic stop or executive exception required for:
- unresolved title defects;
- unsafe structure;
- uninsurable condition without a viable remedy;
- prohibited intended use under zoning with no realistic entitlement path;
- severe environmental contamination;
- insufficient legal access;
- flood/fire/other hazard profile that cannot be economically mitigated;
- utility capacity materially below required use with no feasible upgrade;
- acquisition economics dependent on speculative Bitcoin appreciation;
- mining economics required to rescue a weak property;
- residential plan requiring occupants to share industrial heat/noise conditions.

## Prototype Fit Logic

### Prototype A — NEO House Compact
Best for:
- 3–5 resident houses;
- tiny homes;
- yurts;
- earth homes;
- treehouse homes;
- container homes;
- compact eco-village cottages;
- first apprentice / creator residences.

Preferred:
- 1,800–2,800 sq ft or equivalent modular program;
- garden potential;
- separate shed / utility structure;
- modest rehabilitation requirement.

### Prototype B — NEO House Creator
Best for:
- 6–10 residents;
- large homes;
- small multifamily conversions;
- clustered tiny-home compounds;
- dome homes;
- container compounds;
- modern creator residences.

Preferred:
- 3,500–6,000 sq ft;
- multiple acoustic zones;
- studio / podcast conversion potential;
- courtyard or outdoor production space;
- separated service/compute plant.

### Prototype C — Scholar / Neotherapy House
Best for:
- retreat properties;
- former schools / churches / lodges where lawful and appropriate;
- large residences;
- dome / earth homes;
- yurt clusters;
- courtyard eco-village properties.

Preferred:
- quiet setting;
- library/classroom potential;
- multiple small private rooms;
- garden / walking-path potential;
- low ambient industrial noise.

### Prototype D — NEO Estate Compound
Best for:
- larger acreage;
- multi-building estates;
- farm / ranch conversions;
- eco-village sites;
- mixed clusters of domes, yurts, treehouses, tiny homes, earth homes, cottages and container studios.

Preferred:
- separate public/private/service circulation;
- phased construction potential;
- multiple utility zones;
- strong landscape identity;
- room for detached energy/mining infrastructure.

### Prototype E — NEO Enterprise / Warehouse
Best for:
- operating businesses;
- warehouses;
- light-industrial assets;
- mixed-use commercial sites;
- flex space;
- container-based annex campuses.

Preferred:
- commercial/industrial power;
- loading and service access;
- high ceilings / ventilation;
- office and training areas;
- ability to isolate compute infrastructure from people.

## Detailed Scoring Criteria

### 1. Acquisition Economics — 20
Score:
- price relative to market and replacement cost;
- stabilized NOI / cash-flow potential;
- debt-service coverage;
- seller-finance / creative-finance potential;
- renovation burden;
- taxes / insurance;
- reserve requirement;
- exit flexibility.

5 = strong core economics without mining.
0 = acquisition only works if speculative assumptions are accepted.

### 2. Location & Community Fit — 10
Score:
- safety;
- neighborhood trajectory;
- access to schools, work, services and transportation;
- community compatibility;
- creator/student demand;
- distance to NEO activity nodes.

### 3. Land / Building Adaptability — 10
Score:
- layout flexibility;
- structural adaptability;
- additional dwelling / accessory structure potential;
- modular expansion;
- garden / courtyard potential;
- parking / service access.

### 4. Residential / Program Fit — 10
Score:
- bedroom/bathroom count;
- privacy;
- common spaces;
- circulation;
- life-safety;
- resident capacity;
- accessibility potential.

### 5. Creator / Education Utility — 8
Score:
- studio potential;
- podcast isolation;
- classroom / study space;
- wired network potential;
- storage;
- public/private circulation.

### 6. Neotherapy / Healing Environment — 8
Score:
- quiet;
- daylight;
- landscape;
- air quality;
- ability to create reflection / Neotherapy rooms;
- acoustic privacy;
- separation from mechanical noise.

### 7. Energy & Electrical Capacity — 10
Score:
- service amperage / voltage;
- utility upgrade feasibility;
- tariff;
- solar/battery potential;
- backup power;
- metering options.

### 8. Mining / Compute Suitability — 8
Score:
- power price;
- thermal rejection;
- acoustic isolation;
- dedicated utility area;
- fire/life-safety;
- service access;
- no occupant crossover.

Mining must remain optional to the property thesis.

### 9. Infrastructure & Utilities — 6
Score:
- water;
- sewer/septic;
- broadband;
- roads;
- drainage;
- waste;
- HVAC;
- utility reliability.

### 10. Code / Zoning / Permitting — 5
Score:
- intended use permitted;
- occupancy classification;
- ADU / accessory structure rules;
- business / studio use;
- parking;
- short-term / residential rules where relevant;
- mining / industrial use where relevant.

### 11. Environmental / Resilience — 3
Score:
- flood;
- wildfire;
- wind;
- heat;
- drainage;
- contamination;
- passive design potential;
- climate-appropriate materials.

### 12. Expansion / Replication — 2
Score:
- ability to add units;
- nearby acquisition potential;
- standardization;
- neighborhood / village scalability.
