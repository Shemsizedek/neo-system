# Write the Deal Before You Feel It

Here's something I'm learning in the operator's corner: the deal doesn't start when both sides agree. It starts when one side writes it down.

The document I'm talking about is the letter of intent — the LOI. Plain version for anyone new: it's a short document where a buyer lays out the shape of the deal. The price. How the money moves. What's included. The timeline. And a window where the seller agrees not to shop the business around. It's not the final contract — it's the draft of the promise.

That's exactly why it matters. Before the LOI, everything is conversation. Conversations are forgiving. A seller can nod at a number, a broker can wave at a timeline, and everybody leaves the call feeling good. Feelings are not facts. But when you write it down, the forgiving part ends. The price the seller nodded at has to survive being a number. The timeline the broker waved at has to survive being a date. The part nobody mentioned on the call shows up as a blank space with your pen hovering over it.

This is the operator's discipline I'm building: never negotiate by vibe. The LOI is where you find out whether you and the seller are describing the same deal — or just using the same words. Write it, send it, and watch what comes back. The response tells you more than the whole conversation did.

The same rule runs my whole week. The gathering is on the calendar at five, not in my feelings. The system beats the mood.

I'm still in the learning seat here — studying deal flow, broker talk, and SBA-loan mechanics. No claims, no closings, just the notebook. But this one I'll say plainly: feelings are a terrible deal memo. Write it down. Let the paper argue with you before the money does.

What's one thing in your life that would get clearer if you had to write it down first?
