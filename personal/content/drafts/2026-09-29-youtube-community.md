# Terms Beat Price

The biggest rookie mistake in buying a business — and I'm speaking as the rookie — is staring at the price tag.

When I started studying deal flow, I scrolled listings the way you scroll car ads: filtering by asking price, doing mental math on what I could stretch to afford. Price feels like the deal. It's the headline number.

But the deeper my notebook gets, the more one idea keeps showing up: the price is just one term in the deal. The structure is the rest of it.

Here's the plain-language version. A business at a million can change hands a dozen ways. All cash at closing — clean and rare. Half down, with the seller lending you the rest over a few years — that's a seller note. Or part of the price paid later, tied to how the business performs — an earnout. Same headline number. Completely different deal.

Why it matters: the price is what you agree to pay. The terms are how you survive paying it. A lower price with brutal terms — a huge payment in year one, no cushion if revenue dips — can sink you. A fair price with kind terms can carry you through the learning curve. And when a seller agrees to finance part of the deal, they're saying something: they believe in the business enough to stay in the boat with it. That tells you something no listing will.

So my learner's habit now: read the terms before the price. How much down. Whether the seller will finance. What happens if the numbers dip. The deal is in the details — it always is.

The principle travels past business. Any agreement with a number on it — a job, a partnership — the number is the beginning of the negotiation, not the end.
