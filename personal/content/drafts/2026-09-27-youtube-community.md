# Ask Like It's Your First Sunday

Sunday is the newcomer gathering. New faces, open doors, people walking into a room they don't know yet. I used to treat Sundays like maintenance — show up, be present, hold the space. Lately I'm treating them like a class.

Because newcomers ask the questions the rest of us stopped asking years ago. The simple ones. Why do we gather? What does this hour actually change? Who is it for? Questions with edges we've sanded smooth in our own heads. Sitting with them fresh does something no study block can do.

There's a difference between a student who has been in the room for a year and a student who thinks like it's week one. The first protects what he knows. The second asks what he's missing. Same room, different posture — and the posture is the whole thing.

I've been making this my practice: learn first, then let the newest person in the room rearrange what I thought was settled. If you've been doing something long enough to be bored by it, you're not done with it. You're just done asking about it.

The principle travels past faith. Whatever you're building — your craft, your deal flow, your morning routine — take one thing you think you already understand and question it the way someone walking in for the first time would. Dumb on purpose. Honest on purpose. You might be surprised what opens up.

So my question for you today: what's the newest question you've asked about something old? Drop it below — the beginner question you're almost embarrassed to ask is usually the one worth answering.
