# Keep the System Light

I run my week on systems. The checklist in the morning, the notebook, the study block, the gathering at five. They were supposed to make the day easier to carry. And for a while, they did.

Then I noticed something: every system I build keeps getting heavier. A new list gets added. A tracker gets another column. A ritual gets one more step. I add things with good reasons and I never take anything away. The morning checklist that was supposed to free me now takes longer to complete than the work it was organizing. The notebook has pages I've been carrying for a month and haven't touched. The system designed to serve the work quietly became a second job.

So I added one more practice — and this one is about removing. Once a month, I open every system I run and cut one thing that stopped earning its place. Not the hard parts, not the core rhythm. Just the item that's been sitting there out of habit: the metric I stopped reading, the step I keep skipping, the page I copy forward every month for no reason. If it can't argue for its existence, it goes.

The builder's job isn't only to build. Anything that grows has to be pruned or it stops being useful. The tree gets taller but stops bearing fruit. I've decided I want systems I can carry — light enough to move with, not heavy enough to anchor me.

Maintenance is deletion. That's the whole discipline: add carefully, remove honestly.

Here's what I'm doing today before the gathering at five: opening the notebook, picking one thing to cut, and letting it go. Quietly. No ceremony.

What's one item in your system that's been sitting there too long — and what would happen if you just deleted it?

— Lawiy
