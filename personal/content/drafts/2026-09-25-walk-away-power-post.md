# Walk-Away Power

The most important word in deal-making isn't yes. It's no.

Every shiny deal looks good at first. The numbers glow. The broker is confident. Your brain starts spending the profit before you've finished reading the teaser. That's not analysis — that's appetite.

So I'm learning the discipline of the pass. Not the slow, guilty maybe — the clean no. Three questions before I ever let myself get excited: Why is the owner really selling? What does real cash flow look like after I stop believing the seller's version? What breaks if the owner walks out the door?

If I can't answer those, I don't have a deal. I have a fantasy with a spreadsheet.

Here's what I'm learning in real time: anybody can say yes. Saying yes costs nothing — it's the no that takes discipline. Walking away from the shiny one that doesn't fit is the whole game. The operator who can't pass ends up owning someone else's problems.

I'm not writing this from ten closed acquisitions [to confirm: experience level — frame is learner's]. I'm writing it from the middle of the learning. And the learning is saying: love the no. The no keeps you in the game long enough to earn the right yes.

What's the deal you almost chased — and what stopped you?
