# Learn in the Margins

Friday is the observance. The day runs on its own rhythm, and the study block I protect on other days gets squeezed into whatever's left. For a long time that bothered me. I treat learning like a workout: same time, same chair, same notebook — or it doesn't count.

Then I noticed where most of my real learning actually happens. It isn't the study block. It's the margins. The twenty minutes waiting on a call. The quiet after the observance, when the day's done and the room is still. The stretches I'd normally hand to the phone on reflex.

Here's the learner's discipline I'm running now: stop defending the perfect hour, and start using the stray ones.

One module. One page. One question you actually try to answer instead of scrolling past. A learner in the margins isn't chasing volume — he's collecting completions. Ten focused minutes, repeated across a week, will teach you more than one golden hour you keep postponing because the day got busy. The golden hour is a promise you break; the margin is a minute you actually keep.

There's a second part to this. The margin has an honesty built into it. When you only have fifteen minutes, you can't lie to yourself about the plan. You either open the material or you don't. No ritual, no setup, no ceremony — just the work. That honesty has fixed more of my study habits than any schedule ever did.

So protect the big block where you can. But don't let a busy Friday be an excuse to learn nothing. The margins are where the week actually gets learned.

What are you learning in the margins this week — not the course, the one idea that's stuck?
