# Warm Up Like It Matters

Nobody sees the warm-up. No camera rolls for it, no casting director applauds it. The room is quiet, the mirror is waiting, and the work before the work is the only part that's entirely yours.

I run a simple warm-up before I do anything that counts. Voice first — breath, range, a few minutes of scales until the instrument stops feeling like someone else's. Then body — shake out the tension I'm carrying, find where I hold it, let it go. Then the scene: I read it once through without performing, just listening for what the words actually want.

Here's what the warm-up taught me, and it travels way past acting: presence isn't talent. It's preparation. The reason some people walk into a room and everything settles is rarely charisma — it's that they did the quiet work before the room. Their nerves had somewhere to go. Their mind had a script when the adrenaline showed up.

Most people skip this part. They rehearse the performance and neglect the preparation, then wonder why the performance felt shaky. But confidence has a source, and the source is always earlier than you think. You don't build it on stage. You build it in the empty room at dawn, mirror and all.

I won't pretend I warm up perfectly every time. But I notice the difference immediately. When I do, I walk in already in my body. When I don't, the first ten minutes are me finding myself. The warm-up is the difference between performing and arriving.

So here's my question for you — and a Friday one, gentle: what's the warm-up you keep skipping before the moments that matter? The pause, the breath, the prep you know works but rarely do? Name it. Sometimes naming the ritual is the ritual.
