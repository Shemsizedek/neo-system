# Sit With a Newcomer

Today's the newcomer gathering — the door opens and people walk in who don't know the room yet. I used to treat Sundays like a lighter day. The week's done, Friday was the observance, Monday starts the rhythm again. Sunday was maintenance.

That changed the day I stopped sitting with my usual people and sat with a newcomer instead.

There's a discipline hiding inside the welcome. When you sit beside someone who knows nothing about the room, you start seeing the room through their eyes. The things you take for granted — why we gather, what the hour means, why nobody rushes it — suddenly have to be real to you again, because they're asking the questions you stopped asking years ago. A newcomer doesn't know what's supposed to be routine. That's not their weakness. That's their gift to you.

The other thing the welcome does: it breaks the inward habit. It's easy for a gathering to become a club — the same seats, the same conversations, the same comfort. Sitting with someone new is a small refusal of that drift. You choose the person who doesn't belong yet, and in choosing them, you remember what belonging is actually for.

And here's the quiet part nobody tells you: the newcomer usually leaves with less than you do. You walked in thinking you were giving them the welcome. You walk out with your own walk examined, your own answers renewed, your own gratitude louder. The room that opens its door gets opened back.

So today, wherever your Sunday gathering is — find the newest face and sit down. Ask what brought them. Stay longer than is convenient. The discipline of the welcome will hand you back more than you handed over.
