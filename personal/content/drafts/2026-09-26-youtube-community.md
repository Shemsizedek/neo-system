# Saturday Is the Rehearsal for Sunday

Friday was the anchor. The observance held the week the way an anchor holds a ship — you felt it settle. And Sunday is coming: the newcomer gathering, the open door, people walking in who don't know the room yet.

So what is Saturday? For me it's the in-between day, and I've learned to treat it as the rehearsal. Not a day off from discipline — a different kind of discipline. Friday is reverence. Sunday is welcome. Saturday is preparation.

Preparation doesn't have to be loud. It's the quiet version of care: clearing the mind, checking the heart, making sure the person who walks in tomorrow meets someone who is actually present — not someone still carrying the week's noise. You can't welcome anyone from an empty tank.

I keep Saturday simple. One walk without the phone. One read of something that straightens me out. And one question: is there anything unfinished that's going to follow me into tomorrow? If there is, I finish it or I write it down and let it go. No loose threads at the door of a gathering.

The principle travels past faith. Whatever your Sunday is — the big meeting, the game, the thing that matters — the day before it is part of it. Athletes call it game-day prep. I call it Saturday. The difference between showing up and arriving is usually made the day before.

So here's my question for you, a Saturday one: what's the one thing you could do today that makes tomorrow better for someone else? A call, a cleanup, a clearing of the air. Name it below. Saturday rewards the prepared.
