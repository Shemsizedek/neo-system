# The Rehearsal No One Sees

The audience sees the performance. The performer lives somewhere else entirely.

I've been thinking about this because acting has a visibility problem: everything the public sees is the finished thing. The reel, the read, the booking. Nobody films the warmup. Nobody claps for the sides run one more time at the kitchen table, the voice work on a quiet morning, the tape watched in private with the sound off so you can see what your body is saying.

None of that makes the reel. All of it makes the reel possible.

This is true in acting — I'm learning it through the self-tape discipline, where the private reps are the whole job before the job — and it's true everywhere else I operate. The deal work has its own version. The study block has its own version. Every field has a rehearsal layer nobody sees, and the quality of that layer is what the visible work ends up standing on.

Here's the part that stings a little: the rehearsal is boring by design. That's why it works. It has no audience, no feedback, no applause — which means the only thing running it is your standard. If your private reps are sloppy, your public work is a bet, not a craft. If they're honest, the performance is mostly already decided before anyone's watching.

So I keep one rule now: whatever the room will see, something the room will never see gets to prepare it. The tape before the tape. The rep before the room.

What's one thing in your life that's only good because nobody watched you practice it?

— Lawiy
