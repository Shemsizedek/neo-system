# Every Step Needs One Owner

Every plan I've ever watched stall had the same flaw. Not a missing step — a missing owner. The checklist was complete. The steps were clear. But next to half of them, the owner was "someone." And a step that belongs to someone belongs to no one.

I learned this the slow way, in my own week. The study block nobody defended. The follow-up nobody sent. The gathering prep everybody assumed somebody else handled. None of it failed loudly. It just quietly didn't happen, because three people each thought one of the other two had it.

The builder's fix is boring, and that's why it works: every step gets exactly one name next to it. Not a team. Not a role. A name. If a step has no owner, it doesn't happen. If it has three owners, it still doesn't happen — because each one waits for the other two, and nobody wants to be the one who overstepped.

One name changes everything. It turns "we should" into "I will." It makes the follow-up a promise instead of a wish. And it makes the week honest: when a step has your name on it, you either did it or you didn't. No fog to hide in.

So here's what I'm doing today, before the gathering at five: opening my own systems and checking every step for a name. The ones with "someone" next to them get a real one — mine, or somebody else's, said out loud. The ones with three names get cut down to one.

A system doesn't fail because it's complicated. It fails because nobody owned the next step.

What's one step in your week that's been waiting on "someone" — and what happens if you put your name on it?

— Lawiy
