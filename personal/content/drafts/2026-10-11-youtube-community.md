# The Gathering Goes With You

Sunday is the newcomer gathering. Normally I'd be there — same room, same faces, same work of sitting with whoever walks in the door for the first time.

Today I'm not. Travel has me away from home, so the rhythm that anchors my week — the gatherings Monday through Thursday around five, Friday's observance, Sunday's welcome — doesn't look like itself. For years, a break in the routine meant a break in the discipline. I'd tell myself I'd pick it back up Monday, and by Wednesday the habit was a stranger.

Here's what I'm learning to do instead: take the gathering with me.

The discipline was never the room. The room helped — the people, the hour, the weight of a shared commitment. But underneath it was a skeleton I can carry anywhere: stop at the anchor hour, sit still, turn inward, and do the day's work — the gratitude, the reflection, the resetting of intention. Five o'clock looks different in a hotel room or an unfamiliar house. The pause doesn't have to.

The rule I'm running now is simple. On gathering days, wherever I am at five, the pause happens. Same stillness, same honesty, minus the chairs. Some days it's ten minutes with a notebook and no phone. That's enough. Discipline isn't about keeping the setting perfect; it's about refusing to let the setting be the excuse.

If you're building any rhythm that matters — faith, study, craft — expect the week that breaks your setup. That's the test, not the interruption. The routine that only survives under perfect conditions isn't a discipline yet. It's a preference.

So today, wherever you are, keep the hour. The gathering goes with you.
