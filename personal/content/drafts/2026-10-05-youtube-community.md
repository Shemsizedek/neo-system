# Draw It Before You Build It

Before I write a single page of a plan, I make myself draw it.

One page. Boxes and arrows. The parts, and how they connect. That's it.

I'm building something I call the NEO System — just my open framework for running a life on purpose. And the hardest lesson from building it had nothing to do with discipline or effort. It was this: if I can't draw it, I don't have a system. I have an idea wearing a costume.

Ideas are forgiving. They live in your head, where everything connects beautifully and nothing ever breaks. A paragraph can hide a gap the size of a doorway. But a drawing has nowhere to hide. When you put the parts in boxes and draw the arrows between them, the missing piece announces itself. That step you assumed would "just happen"? It's a blank space with no arrow. The part everything else depends on? The arrows pile up — you just found the bottleneck before it cost you a month.

Here's the practice now: every project gets one page before it gets a plan. The evening routine, the study block, the deal review — draw first. What feeds what. What breaks first. What's decoration. If the drawing looks simple, good — simple is what survives a real Tuesday. If it's tangled, that's good too — you just found the tangle before it found you.

The builder's mistake is confusing the document with the build. A beautiful plan is not a system. A system is the thing that keeps working when you're tired, distracted, or bored — and the drawing is where you prove it can, before it costs you a dollar or a day.

Try it this week: one page, boxes and arrows, whatever you're working on. If it won't draw, it won't run. Go back until it does.
